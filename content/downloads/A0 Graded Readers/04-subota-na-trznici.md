# Subota na tržnici 🍅🇭🇷
*Saturday at the Market*

## Croatian story 🇭🇷

Subota je. 🌤️ Ana je na tržnici. Tržnica je velika i lijepa.

Ana vidi rajčice 🍅, jabuke 🍎 i kruh. 🥖

"Dobro jutro! Što želite?" kaže prodavačica.

"Dobro jutro! Koliko su rajčice?" pita Ana.

"Dva eura. Rajčice su svježe, a kruh je topao!" kaže prodavačica.

"Super! Tri rajčice, dvije jabuke i kruh, molim."

Prodavačica daje rajčice, jabuke i kruh. "Izvolite!"

"Hvala! Koliko je to?" pita Ana.

"Pet eura," kaže prodavačica.

Ana daje novac.

"Hvala! Doviđenja!" kaže prodavačica.

"Doviđenja!" kaže Ana.

Ana je sretna. Rajčice su svježe, a jabuke su dobre. Subota je lijepa. 😊

## English translation 🇬🇧

It is Saturday. 🌤️ Ana is at the market. The market is big and lovely.

Ana sees tomatoes 🍅, apples 🍎 and bread. 🥖

"Good morning! What would you like?" says the seller.

"Good morning! How much are the tomatoes?" asks Ana.

"Two euros. The tomatoes are fresh, and the bread is warm!" says the seller.

"Great! Three tomatoes, two apples and bread, please."

The seller gives the tomatoes, apples and bread. "Here you go!"

"Thank you! How much is that?" asks Ana.

"Five euros," says the seller.

Ana gives the money.

"Thank you! Goodbye!" says the seller.

"Goodbye!" says Ana.

Ana is happy. The tomatoes are fresh, and the apples are good. Saturday is lovely. 😊

## Useful Croatian patterns 🧩

**1. Plural of feminine nouns: *-a* becomes *-e***
- **rajčica → rajčice**, **jabuka → jabuke**. Feminine adjectives follow: **svježa → svježe**, **dobra → dobre**.
- The accusative plural is the same form, so *Ana vidi rajčice* uses it too.
- Examples: *Rajčica je svježa.* (The tomato is fresh.) *Rajčice su svježe.* (The tomatoes are fresh.) *Jabuke su dobre.* (The apples are good.)

**2. *je* and *su*: "is" and "are"**
- **je** goes with one thing, **su** with several. You have met *Koliko je to?* and *Koliko je kava?* Now: *Koliko su rajčice?* (How much are the tomatoes?)
- Examples: *Kava je topla.* (The coffee is warm.) *Jabuke su jeftine.* (The apples are cheap.)

**3. Numbers with nouns: *dva / dvije, tri***
- After **dva** (masculine, neuter), **dvije** (feminine) and **tri**, the noun ends in **-e** for feminine nouns: *dvije jabuke, tri rajčice*. Masculine: *dva eura*.
- From **pet** upward the noun changes again (*pet rajčica*). Learn that form later; for now, *pet eura* works as a phrase.
- Examples: *Dvije kave, molim.* (Two coffees, please.) *Tri karte, molim.* (Three tickets, please.)

**4. At a place: *na* + locative, *na tržnici***
- Feminine nouns change **-a** to **-i** in the locative: **tržnica → na tržnici**, **stanica → na stanici**. To say where you go, **na** + accusative: *Idem na tržnicu.*
- Examples: *Ana je na tržnici.* (Ana is at the market.) *Ana je na stanici.* (Ana is at the stop.) *Idem na tržnicu.* (I'm going to the market.)

## Quick comprehension check 🎯

**1. Što želi Ana? / What does Ana want?**
- a) Tri rajčice, dvije jabuke i kruh / Three tomatoes, two apples and bread
- b) Kruh i kavu / Bread and coffee
- c) Dva kolača / Two cakes

**2. Koliko je to? / How much is it?**
- a) Dva eura / Two euros
- b) Pet eura / Five euros
- c) Tri eura / Three euros

**3. Što znači "svježe"? / What does "svježe" mean?**
- a) Big / Big
- b) Cheap / Cheap
- c) Fresh / Fresh

## Answer key

1. **a**: Ana kaže: "Tri rajčice, dvije jabuke i kruh, molim."
2. **b**: Prodavačica kaže: "Pet eura."
3. **c**: "Svježe" znači "fresh."
